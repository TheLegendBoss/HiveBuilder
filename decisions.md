# Decisions

Quick reference. `docs/design.md` has the full text and reasoning.

## Locked rules

**Combat**
- Sump squid: tentacles have 2 HP and cap at 3 per lane. It generates a tentacle when it acts in a lane with no tentacle, but not once that lane is closed (it can still attack there, without new tentacles; the body attacks instead, 2 damage is a placeholder). When a tentacle attacks, another tentacle in the lane attacks for free, so clear lanes rather than hammering an open one.
- Monsters have one large pooled body HP plus lane HP. Units screen the lane, you defeat the lane HP, and only then can you hit the body from that lane. The monster is defeated when its body reaches 0.
- 3 fixed lanes; win by overwhelming 2 of 3. Units screen their lane's HP. Captured lanes let units attack adjacent lanes; the enemy can still deploy into a captured lane and its HP never recovers. No cap on units per lane for either side (extra bodies cost deploy actions).
- Alternating single actions; a round is one action from each side. Setup: first 3 rounds deploy one unit per lane, no attacks.
- Downed members do not screen their lane and cannot act. Healing can stand them up mid-fight; after a fight they rise at 1 HP. Soak gains cannot exceed an armor's maximum. Armor soak refills between fights; built-in soak is not refilled mid-fight.
- Slots: two weapon slots per member (one-handed takes one, two-handed takes both, shields take a weapon slot), an accessory slot for tools, armor, implant and perk. Weapon classes set the default hand count: one-handed are sidearms, close, thrown explosives, shields and light melee; two-handed are line, support, precision, heavy melee and special melee. Light and heavy variants can flip it (a light carbine is 1H, a breaching shotgun is 2H). Cuffs are accessories; a cuff-firing weapon is a special weapon. The Concord Warden carries a light flechette carbine (1H) and a bolas launcher (1H special) that fires the Cuff Shot cards.
- Weapon slots: humanoids have 2 primary, 1 backup and up to 2 more from implants and backpacks; a backpack has its own slot; no deck size limit (junk dilution is its own cost). A backup weapon adds only one card of your choice to the deck, picked when you equip it. Large droid chassis up to 6; melee droid built-in melee plus a two-handed backup slot; cult faction third arm.
- Implants (preliminary): lasting injuries are lasting and an implant fixes them (prosthetic swaps the penalty for a negative card; types are prosthetic, cybernetic, bionic, archeotech). A lasting injury opens a separate prosthetic slot that exists only while the injury does and never competes with other implants. White implants come from fabricator buildings, prosthetics for Eagles at clinics and hospitals; implants also drop as loot. Humanoids only; droids repair instead. Implants have body areas (limb, eye, brain, torso) and an injury needs the matching area; one prosthetic slot per lasting injury, no cap. Full catalogue and the area map for Spinal, Grievous and Enfeebled held.
- Shields are defense gear: in the starter CSVs the shield cards (Glass shield, Mantlet, Combat shield) are tagged defense instead of armor. A shield still takes a weapon slot and adds its own max soak.
- Grenades come two ways: reusable gear that takes a weapon slot (a one-handed grenade belt with an ammo counter like other guns, or a grenade launcher), or consumables carried in the belt slot as one-offs that cost no weapon slot.
- Class templates: light melee is a cheap fast attack with a defensive utility (Parry), heavy melee a wind-up big hit with a lane utility (Cleave), special melee one signature trick per tool. Melee fight-back is a fixed rule that gear can change (Parry-type utilities and shields cancel or reduce it, some weapons improve the swing back). Tools: physical act on the squad and room (and can be used up or run on counters), digital act on units and systems (and run on Tech checks); tools also act as keys outside fights and the right tool can replace an attribute requirement at a gate.
- Rarity: red is Borderlands/Diablo-style named uniques that can appear more than once with different riders and stats; gold is archeotech or lostech (working). Red and gold can appear in late slots of any pack tier with a tiny chance. Fuel for red and gold stays at 5; gold and red pitch are both 32. Cards can be locked in the inventory to stop accidental pitching, burning or use. Backpacks and implants can add a third or fourth weapon slot; a large droid chassis can carry up to 6; a melee droid has built-in melee plus a two-handed backup slot; a cult faction can have a third arm.
- Supplies: the ration clock ticks on movement only, not on fights. Ammo counters reload both ways (an ammo card mid-fight, and counters refill between rooms). Inventory is one shared squad bag plus a personal carry limit per member.
- Not combat, but fixed: currency is Eagles. Pitch values 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 (white to orange). An upgrade costs 4x the target tier's pitch value, same-item copies count double. A standard 5-card pack costs about one room's income. Members have 5 tiers; XP from fights and from completing dungeons. Crew heal fully by the next expedition; lasting injuries do not heal on a timer, they stay until an implant fixes them.
- Armor: one armor piece per member; a shield counts as a second piece and adds its own max soak. Max soak by rarity per piece: white and green 3, blue and purple 4, orange 5 (faction starting gear can differ). Better rarity brings better armor cards (slowly rising refills, riders). Accessories (kevlar undersuit, silent bodysuit) can complement armor; body-part armor slots may be revisited.
- Fixed damage, no misses. Armor is an absorb pool (soak). Working soak numbers: built-in 1 for small chassis, 2 for large chassis; 3 per armor piece (vanguard 6), +1 enemy damage against every force. Melee triggers a free immediate fight-back.
- Difficulty target for the TSC starting kit (2 Repair Packs): about 85 to 90% of dungeons finished at 5 rooms and 60% at 10 (playtest 06 gets 86% and 66%).
- Fight length: short 8-10 rounds, medium 14-18, long 24+.

**Cards**
- Every card is an effect and fuel (1 by default; supplies carry more; fuel value scales with rarity, 1 for white up to 5 for orange, because burning a better card is a bigger sacrifice). Burn = discard from hand to pay for gear actions.
- One shared squad hand, drawn back to max at the start of each action. Each faction sets its own baseline hand size (the three starters use 7 for now). Discard and draw discards up to 2 cards; skills and upgrades can raise that.
- One activation per gear. Gear adds actions plus signature cards (template: 2 core + 1 utility).
- Fates: Discard, Exhaust, Pitch (permanent), Consumed (disposable gear).
- Checks: an exhausted card adds fuel value plus its Check-line bonus on the matching attribute. No player dice.

**Hive and economy**
- A dungeon has 5 to 10 fights. The squad heals fully when the dungeon ends, and healing supplies (cards that double as fuel) keep it going in between. Healing supplies heal 4 HP; the TSC starts with two Repair Packs of its own (32-card deck), and the Pressmen Ration Pack and Concord Field Dressing also heal 4.
- Floor generation keeps going after the second gate while 3 or more exits are open. Floors are grouped by position, with sections per floor: Very High 1-3 (easy start), High 2-4, Middle 3-5, Lower 4-6, Ground 5-8 (very hard), Sub 9-15, Deep 5-8, Extremely Deep 4-6 (working names). Gates ask for one requirement type on early floors, a mix in the middle, and two at once far down; gate power cost scales with floor depth.
- Win condition depends on the gang; the generic one is to hold a set number of districts or claim the Spire. Campaign modes: tutorial (short), Campaign (medium), open-ended play (intended, you set hive size).
- Stress comes from both the world (dark rooms, ambushes, long expeditions, crew downed) and opt-in gambles. Some clog cards have a damage-on-discard drawback (burn costs 1 damage, stress costs nothing). The results deck reshuffles when it runs out; a game setting can also reshuffle it each expedition.
- Packs: one long 15-slot ramp with stacking step-up chances; tiers change only the steepness (no guaranteed floors, any slot has a low chance of a good pull); a 5-card pack is the first 5 slots (cheap filler or fuel), the 15-card pack is the real buy. Five tiers (working names Basic, Standard, Veteran, Elite, Eagle Pack), plus a reward-only Vault Pack (quest, gamble or reward, not buyable) that sits between Elite and Eagle Pack. Step-up chances are 20% higher across the board than the first draft. Eagle 15-pack costs about 11.4 rooms. Red/gold chance in a 15-card pack: Elite 1.7%, Vault 5%, Eagle 10% (model in tools/pack_odds.py; placeholder numbers, left as is until playtest, including the 85% per-slot cap and the strong Eagle 5-pack). A Basic 5-pack costs 1 room's income, a Basic 15-pack 2.25 rooms, each tier up costs 1.5x more (15-packs are cheaper per card). Single-item packs (like a 15-card Autogun pack) ramp over one item's rarities and variants, and can include even rarer versions. Specialty packs are tiered too; each step deeper in scope (perks, then a role or type of perk, then one specific perk) multiplies the price by 1.75.
- Downed members' gear and cards stay in play, so others can burn them as fuel or play them.
- Humanoid aftermath deck: 35 cards (see `data/aftermath_deck_humanoid.csv`): 20 short-term or none, 13 lasting, 1 Critical Injury (dies unless treated at a clinic), 1 Memorable Death. All 13 lasting results stay until an implant fixes them; the old Grievous Injury clinic Nerve-check downgrade is gone. Droids get their own mostly-repairable list (still to write).
- Enemy forces: depth scales both threat tier and gear. Gang rosters come from the gang engine. Non-gang forces mix three styles (fixed cards, tiered versions that strengthen with depth, random gear from a depth-based table); monsters scale with depth too. Most gear drops on a 4+ on a d6 and gang equipment on a 3+, before passive-tree upgrades (the d6 is to be mapped to a results deck draw).
- Floors fill from room, encounter, denizens and loot decks. Rooms have up to 3 exits from the results deck.
- Hierarchy: strata, districts, floors, sections. Gates, sections and footholds are claimed with power (gang influence).
- Supplies (rations counter plus ammo and healing cards) are the expedition clock.
- Rarity ladder: white, green, blue, purple, orange; red (named) and gold (archeotech) above.

**Factions**
- TSC (Tenebrae Security Combine): droids, forgiving recovery through brain chips and Eagle repair; Eagle hunger is the cost.
- Pressmen Union: heavy-gravity-world smelter labor (not vat-grown), enters at the foundry strata. Faction rule is Solidarity: Shoulder to Shoulder (sharing a lane with another Pressman gives +1 soak per round and +1 melee damage) and Union Grudge (when a Pressman is downed, the others deal +1 damage for the rest of the fight, no stacking). Cost is ration hunger (working rate 1.5x). Heavy weapons keep a 1-round wind-up. Starting crew of 4.
- Concord Marshals: our own upper-hive court-and-collections service (not a street police force), enters from above, squad of 3 elite members. Faction rule is Warrant: Warrant Mark (one marked enemy, +1 damage from your units), Subdue (a unit taken to 0 by a Subdue attack is arrested, not killed) and Tag and Cuff (Exposed, then Stun on cuff). Cost is Authority, a campaign track that rises with arrests and bounties and falls when a Warranted unit is killed; writ gates open on Authority instead of power. Buildings: Holding Cells and Bounty Office.
- More bodies cost deploy actions that could have been attacks (except for the two horde factions), so bigger squads trade tempo for synergies like Solidarity.
- Starter balance accepted (playtest 09): TSC 82% / 69% / 64% finish at 5 / 8 / 10 rooms, Pressmen 92% / 78% / 66%, Concord 89% / 76% / 71%. The TSC stays as is (armor soak 3 per piece, 2 Repair Packs of 4 HP). Small known gap: a non-gun unit's pistol-whip should be melee and draw a fight-back, which the TSC sim does not model.
- Pressmen update (playtest 08): four crew, working starting HP 10 each (40 total) and 10 total armor soak (Ironback 4, the others 2), 29-card deck with 2 Ration Packs. They are meant to be mostly HP; healing and rests are strengthened later by a gang passive.
- Concord update (playtest 08): four starting crew (Marshal, Sentinel, Warden, Breacher), 26 HP total, armor soak 4 per piece. Subdue is replaced by Concussive: stun counters instead of HP loss, ignoring soak. Stunned at counters at least current HP, captured at twice current HP. A stunned enemy rolls to remove counters (4 or more removes 1). Stun counters also raise enemy fuel costs (not yet simulated). Volley is modal (3 lethal or 5 concussive), Cuff Shot (x3) splashes 4 on one enemy and 2 on the other in its lane, Stun Pulse gives 2 counters to the lane, Breach Blast is 4 lethal. Warrant Mark applies to a monster's body (+1 damage, never captured). Enemy fight-backs get the +1 enemy damage.
- Gang engine: AI gangs with 4 difficulties and 4 attitudes (Raiders, Builders, Swarm, Cadre), each with 3 paths.

## Open questions

- [x] Floor generation: Decided: a floor is generated one section at a time using the floor group's section count, gates sit between sections and floors, each section has at least one way up and one way down, and section size comes from the room deck size and the number of room connectors, so sections grow as you add rooms from packs. The starter room deck is 21 cards (8 Fight, 4 Scavenge, 2 Gamble, 2 Hazard, 1 Market, 1 Rest, 1 Clinic, 2 Gate), and the mix after that depends on what you pull. An expedition can cover one section, several, or a whole floor, but a normal expedition tries to clear and claim a section. Gates ask for one requirement type early, a mix in the middle and two far down. Keys come from the loot deck, from bosses or guarded rooms, and from markets. Gate power cost scales with depth, working from about 1 on easy floors up to about 6 on the deepest, and it falls mostly on floor gates. A section ends when both of its gates are placed and no open exits remain. Fights per dungeon grow with the room deck (5 to 10 is the starter range). Attribute thresholds scale with floor group, and room cards have rarity. If the room deck runs out while exits are still open, the leftover exits are sealed as dead ends and the section ends (a reshuffle was considered and set aside). Rarer room cards are stronger versions of the same type (a rare Market stocks better packs, a rare Rest restores more) and also unlock room types white rooms cannot be, such as Vault, Lair or boss, and Bounty from the held list. Section gates cost no power: they ask for an attribute or a key only.
- [x] Floor groupings: Decided: eight tiers mapped to four strata (upper hive 1 and 2, middle hive 3 and 4, lower hive 5 and 6, sump 7 and 8), with the Spire a special final district above tier 1 that appears once enough lower districts are claimed. The in-world names in the hive and floor map section are kept (Gilded Terraces, Upper Galleries, the Midways, Cinder Rows, Groundworks, the Undercroft, the Deeps and the Maw), each with a tier number for tracking. A floor's section count is random within its range and can be stretched by the setup hive size. The Undercroft is as hard as the Groundworks but sprawling, and the Deeps and the Maw have endless sections (generated only for that expedition, incredibly difficult, increased rewards). The Deeps are harder than the Groundworks and the Maw is harder than the Deeps. Endless sections are entered through an optional special gate at the end of a normal section, have no end, and are left by retreating, with rewards growing per section cleared. Retreating from an endless section is free at a checkpoint after every cleared section, and anywhere else it carries the normal retreat penalty. Rewards escalate: each section is worth more than the last, so going deeper pays off more than stopping early.
- [ ] Crafting numbers: Decided: pitch 1, 2, 4, 8, 16; upgrade cost 4x the target tier's pitch value with same-item copies counting double; currency Eagles; pack prices are set (see Packs). Gold and red pitch are both 32, and cards can be locked. Economy: Decided: one room's income is about 10 Eagles (Basic 5-pack 10 Eagles, 15-pack about 23; Standard 15 and 34; Veteran about 23 and 51; Elite 34 and 76; Eagle 51 and 114). Selling a card pays 1 Eagle per pitch point. Income varies a lot by depth. Repairs are small; buildings, promotions and training are the biggest sinks. At 1 Eagle per pitch point, selling a whole 15-card pack returns 85% of a Basic pack's price (69% Standard, 59% Veteran, 47% Elite, 45% Eagle). Promotion costs 20, 40, 80 and 160 Eagles for tiers 2 to 5 (doubling), and training is the promotion cost with no separate fee (probably). Buildings are cheap to start and each upgrade costs more (working 30, 60 and 120 Eagles), and a better building needs its card from a pack, so upgrades cannot be bought without it. Depth income curve: working numbers to test and tweak in play, a tier 8 room paying about 5 times a tier 1 room on a steady curve (10, 16, 21, 27, 33, 39, 44 and 50 Eagles per room for tiers 1 to 8). Still open: base building costs and the droid repair and chassis numbers.
- [x] Unit tiers: Decided: 5 tiers, XP from fights and from completing dungeons. XP thresholds double with each tier (working 20, 40, 80, 160), fights give XP by threat tier plus a dungeon bonus and a depth multiplier, the pace target is tier 2 after 1 to 2 expeditions and tier 5 after 15 to 20, every member on the expedition earns a share and those who fought earn more, and promotion costs XP, Eagles and a card. Recovery: Decided: crew heal fully by the next expedition, and lasting injuries do not heal on a timer: all 13 lasting results stay until an implant fixes them (a Critical Injury must still be treated at a clinic before the next expedition). Droids: Decided: droid tiers and chassis tiers add together, and a destroyed droid's brain chip is recovered automatically on a win (the repair costs Eagles) or by a results draw if the droid is left behind. The droid aftermath list is written (data/aftermath_deck_droid.csv). A minor repair costs 1 Eagle times the chassis tier and a major repair costs 3 times the chassis tier (1 and 3 at tier 1, 5 and 15 at tier 5). Each chassis tier adds 2 HP and one perk slot every two tiers. The minor and major split in the droid aftermath data file is accepted.
- [ ] Power (gang influence): Decided: power is a level that rises with your total influence, and each level grants one passive tree point; the tree will have many more nodes per branch than the placeholder five. A separate spendable pool pays for gates and footholds. It is earned by claiming sections and districts, by clearing fights and completing dungeons, and by holding control rooms (income per expedition). Working rate: an expedition that claims a section earns about as much as one early floor gate costs, so power is a steady drip. Losses, such as losing a claimed section to a rival or a failed expedition, take the spendable pool first; if it is empty, the level can drop and a tree point is refunded or locked. A foothold costs power scaled to the size of the section. Control rooms are key critical infrastructure: each section type has one, it is added to the room deck when you enter a section and can never be drafted, and it has to be won and then paid for with power. A section type combines a floor theme (which sets the look) and a function (which sets the control room). Winning and paying for the control room is how you establish the foothold. What a control room gives depends on its theme and can be power income each expedition, a building type it unlocks, a section-wide bonus or a first-capture reward. It is mostly won by a boss or elite fight, but some section types use a different challenge. Still open: the list of section functions and their control rooms, and the numbers. Buildings go in claimed rooms, so there is no separate home base.
- [ ] Armor details: Decided: one armor piece per member, shields add their own max soak, max soak by rarity (white and green 3, blue and purple 4, orange 5), armor cards improve with rarity, and armor refills between fights. Classes: Decided (melee, tool and implant templates and implant body areas are recorded under Class templates and Implants). Still held: the full implant catalogue and the injury-to-area map for Spinal, Grievous and Enfeebled. Held for a later pass: implants are a large topic, so only a preliminary review is wanted for now. A faction's starting gear can differ from the soak scale (confirmed).
- [x] Battlefield details: Decided: the battlefield is drawn from the results deck. When a fight starts, one card is drawn per lane. The suit sets the kind of environmental modifier (working: clubs cover, diamonds hazards, hearts elevation, spades concealment) and the rank sets its level, from ace as the lowest (few) to king as the highest (many), with no special face-card effects. A lane gets one card and one modifier by default, though some rooms modify this. The level is pure luck and does not scale with depth. Modifiers affect both sides in the lane equally. Cover (clubs): units in the lane gain extra soak that refills each round, and the lane gains extra lane HP (both effects, level sets the amount; working). Hazards (diamonds): every unit in the lane, on either side, takes damage equal to the level each round (working). Elevation (hearts): the first side to deploy a unit in the lane claims the high ground and gets bonus damage on its ranged attacks. Concealment (spades; fog, smoke, darkness): every ranged attack into or out of the lane costs extra fuel equal to the level, and melee ignores it, so it taxes ranged play instead of absorbing damage like cover does and gives melee units a counter. Traps (hidden until triggered, set off only by the side that deploys second) are kept as a fallback if concealment is dropped. Rank maps to level in three bands: ace to 5 is level 1, 6 to 9 is level 2, and 10 to king is level 3. Cover keeps both its soak and lane HP effects for now, to tune in playtests. Battlefield cards come off the top of the same results deck players track. Some rooms change the draw: dark rooms always have concealment, flooded rooms work like concealment but for melee attacks, and in big rooms the center lane draws two cards. Deploy order is an initiative draw: each side draws a card from the top of the results deck, and the higher card deploys first and so claims the high ground. It is one draw per fight, and ties break by suit order. Suits never shift values. Suit order from highest to lowest is spades, hearts, diamonds, clubs. A dark or flooded room's modifier stacks with the drawn one, and its level comes from the rank of the card drawn for that lane, so a 7 of clubs in a dark room is level 2 cover plus level 2 concealment (working reading; a spade in a dark room is simply concealment at its level, not doubled). Unit HP tiers (grunts 3 to 4, specialists 5 to 6, leaders about 8) are a working estimate.
